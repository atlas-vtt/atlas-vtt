## New

- **Party**: a collection's settings get a Party tab for the players at your table: a name, a picture and a colour each. The initiative tracker's party button adds them all at once, without tokens on the map; Roll initiative leaves them out and a click on a player's card takes the number they rolled. The tab also sets up the picture an enemy added with Add enemy starts with.
- **More from the initiative tracker's options**: Add enemy adds an enemy without a token, named Enemy 1, Enemy 2 and so on, on the opponents' side. Add combatant opens the library, and a token or encounter opened there joins the fight beside the map. Reset initiative ends the fight and empties every number and condition, keeping the combatants for the next roll. Compact view shows the tracker as a plain list without portraits. The tracker can stand at an edge of its own, apart from the players' list.
- **Card menu**: give any combatant who isn't on the players' side a colour (opponents start in red), which both lists show; hide any combatant from the players' list without hiding its token on the map.
- **Conditions from the tracker**: a combatant's card menu gives and takes away the map's conditions, also for combatants without tokens. A condition can last a number of the combatant's own turns; the badge counts them down and the condition ends at 0. Stepping back a turn counts them back up.
- **The players' initiative lists**: they can stand at any edges at once, each turned to face the players at its edge, for a table TV. A list across the top or bottom runs as a row, with the round's name riding along under its cards, and moves on like a conveyor: who acted slides out at the front and comes in again at the end. The lists slide in when a fight starts and out when it ends.
- **Turn banners on the player view**: a fight's start and end get banners of their own, every turn banner's border flashes in the combatant's colour, and the turn's card on the list flashes and can pulse. The player view settings set how large the list and the banner are, how long the banner stands, how many cards each list shows, and whether names show without nameplates. The banner can stand at each list, on its side towards the table, instead of in the middle, or the table can dim instead while the cards move on.
